The Seven Hermetic Principles.

A reading, followed by a question.

The app includes the seven principles as presented in The Kybalion. These are philosophical and metaphysical teachings. The notes below are brief interpretations for reflection, rather than the original passages or a claim that the teachings have been scientifically established.

You can work with an idea, disagree with it, or set it aside. Start with the one that has some bearing on a question you already have.

Mentalism

The book begins with mind as fundamental to reality. For personal reflection, start closer to home: the story you tell about an event changes how you experience it. That does not mean you caused everything that happened to you.

What happened, and what have I added to it in my telling?

Correspondence

A pattern seen at one scale may help you notice one at another. A conversation you keep avoiding might resemble the way you keep putting off a decision. Follow the resemblance and see where it leads.

Where else do I recognize this pattern?

Vibration

The text describes existence as movement. As a reflective idea, it invites attention to change: what seemed fixed yesterday may already be shifting. Pay attention to the movement before deciding what it means.

What is changing that I am still treating as settled?

Polarity

The book treats apparent opposites as differences of degree. A judgment such as “I’m either confident or afraid” can hide the mixed state you actually inhabit. Naming the degrees gives you more to work with.

Am I turning a difference of degree into an either/or?

Rhythm

The principle attends to ebb and flow. The app returns to it through cycles and intentions: a difficult afternoon need not describe your whole week. Revisit what you wrote when you have rested, and see what remains true.

What keeps returning, and what changes when it returns?

Cause and Effect

The book asks readers to notice causes rather than treating events as isolated. In a guided exercise, that can mean following a question past its first answer, then separating the conditions you can change from those you cannot.

What can I influence here, and what is outside my control?

Gender

The historical text uses masculine and feminine language for complementary processes, including initiating and receiving. That is the book’s vocabulary, not a rule about someone’s identity, body, or social role. You can examine the idea without accepting its categories.

Does this situation need more initiative, more receptiveness, or a different question altogether?

Source: The Kybalion, by Three Initiates, especially Chapter II. This book is one route into the wider Hermetic tradition.
