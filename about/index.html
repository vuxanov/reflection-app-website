A way to see yourself more clearly.

VD is developed by Dimitar Vuksanov, an independent product and UX designer.

It brings together an interest in psychology, philosophy and esoteric thought with a practical question: how can a personal space help us ask better questions and understand ourselves over time?

The answer is a place to write, work through structured reflections, set intentions and explore different perspectives.

VD is pronounced “VEE-dee,” like vidi. The name is inspired by Slavic words for seeing—a connection to the idea of understanding yourself.

Understanding yourself across cycles.

The cycles framework was developed for VD. It organizes reflection across nested timescales: from the rhythm of the next few hours to the wider perspective of seasons and years.

Some questions belong to the moment. Others become clearer when you return to them over a week, a month or a season. The framework gives those different perspectives a place in the same practice.

In the app, cycles connect to intention-setting. You consider what matters for a period of time, put it into your own words and return to it later. The core flow works with hours, days, weeks and months; the readings place these within a broader seasonal and yearly picture.

This is a framework for reflection. Its symbolic relationships are not a system for predicting your mood or health.

From a wider perspective to a smaller step.

Looking at a longer cycle can help you consider what you want to make room for. Looking at a shorter one brings the question back to what you can do now. You decide what is useful; the framework gives you a structure to work with.

More than one way to ask a question.

Psychology offers ways to examine thoughts, emotions and behavior. Philosophy opens questions about purpose and how to live. Symbolic and esoteric traditions offer another language for reflection.

VD places these perspectives alongside each other. It doesn’t ask you to accept one explanation for everything, or treat a philosophical principle as a scientific finding.

The space should feel like yours.

Appearance is part of the experience. Elegant uses softer forms and serif headings. Brutalist uses monospace type and sharper geometry. Both have light and dark modes.

Accent colors and symbols let you change the feel without changing the reflection itself. You can keep things quiet, choose a different visual language, or follow your iPhone’s light and dark settings.

AI is an option, not a requirement.

The starting point is your own attention. The questions, writing and readings work without AI. If you choose to use the guide, the model runs on your iPhone.

VD is free for iPhone, with no account, subscription or in-app purchases. It is still in development. Screenshots show the actual current app, including its earlier AI Guru name.
